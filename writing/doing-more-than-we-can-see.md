# Doing more than we can see

> We are about to do far more than anyone can see. And still want to be the one in charge.

That's the line at the top of my homepage now. Getting there took a while, and most of the drafts were wrong in useful ways. This is the thinking behind it.

## Output is about to stop being the constraint

For most of history, how much work got done was limited by how many people were doing it. One person produced one person's worth of output. That limit is going away. What one person can produce is about to scale by something like a hundred times, and most of that work will have an effect before anyone else has looked at it.

Most people already believe that part. The more interesting question is what it does to everything around the work.

## Checking everything stops being an option

The instinctive answer to more output is more checking: review it, verify it, sign it off. That works while the volume stays human-sized. It doesn't survive a hundredfold increase. There will be too much work, and it will be too complex to verify piece by piece. Any process that depends on someone looking at everything will either slow the work back down or quietly become a rubber stamp.

So I don't think verification is the answer. My early drafts said things like "nobody can check everything anymore." That's true, but it describes the problem from the wrong end. Reading and checking were never the point.

## Engineers already learned this

One thing I learned as an engineer is that you don't trust a system because you've read every line of code in it. Nobody has. You trust it because you built the means to know when it's wrong. That means tests that fail, monitoring that alerts, invariants that have to hold, and the ability to trace what happened and roll it back.

Moving from inspecting everything to building the means to know is what let software grow beyond what any one person could hold in their head. Soon that lesson will apply to all work, not just software.

## Assurance, not control

It would be easy to tell this as a story about losing control. I don't think it is one. The goal isn't to clamp down on all this new output. The goal is to be able to stand behind it.

Assurance is what lets you go further. A team that trusts its deployment pipeline ships more often. An organisation that knows, with confidence, that what was done in its name was sound can afford to let far more get done. That's what I mean by staying in charge. You don't watch every move. You know things are under control without seeing everything.

## Not just a company problem

I started from large organisations, because that's where I work and where this will be felt first. Thousands of people already act under one name, and audit exists because nobody at the top can see it all. I think audit becomes far more important in this world. It also has to change shape. It will be less about checking samples after the fact and more about building the means to know.

The drafts that said "companies" still felt too narrow. This applies to all of us. Anyone about to get a hundred times more done will face the same problem at their own scale. That's why the headline says "we".

## Where that leaves the line

*We are about to do far more than anyone can see.* That's the shift.

*And still want to be the one in charge.* That's the part that doesn't change.

With output scaling 100x, we have to rethink how we know things are under control. The answer won't be seeing everything. It will be building the means to know when something is wrong.
